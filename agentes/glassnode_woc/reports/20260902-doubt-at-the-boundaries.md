## Executive Summary
- The August short-squeeze rally pushed spot into the **$80K** zone before hitting expected overhead supply and stalling.
- Retesting the same price seen in May now shows **68% of supply in profit**, up from **65%** in May — summer redistribution has left more coins in a position to sell at the same nominal price.
- Spot ETFs pulled in **$290M/day** at the peak of the rally, but secondary market turnover stayed muted around **$3B/day**, a pattern typical of headline-driven flows rather than durable demand.
- The US 10-Year yield round-tripped back to **4.8%** (fresh cycle highs) in just **eight trading sessions**, fully erasing the relief from the mid-August Treasury buyback announcement.
- Short-dated options skew spiked with the squeeze and has since round-tripped back toward neutral; the **September 25 quarter-end expiry** now anchors roughly **$14B** in open interest.

## Squeeze Aftermath — Rejection Below the Wall
The August 19 short squeeze cleared out leverage but ran straight into a dense supply zone, as flagged in the prior edition. That played out this week: price broke above **$80K** on August 27, met resistance, and reversed down to **$76K**, triggering a run of long liquidations. The liquidation map is now bilateral — a dense pocket of short liquidations sits between **$83K and $86K** overhead, while a still-intact band of long liquidation fuel sits between **$60K and $63K** below. Spot is currently boxed between the two.

## On-Chain Supply — The Profit Overhang
Comparing two visits to roughly the same price level exposes a shift in the profitability of the coin base: near **$78K** in May, about **65%** of supply sat in profit; at the same price in late August, that share had risen to **68%**. The gain traces to summer accumulation that pushed the Short-Term Holder cost basis up to roughly **$71K**, meaning more coins now flip profitable at the same nominal price — a larger pool of potential sellers waiting at prior highs.

## Defined Range Boundaries
Blending on-chain cost-basis models with the coin distribution profile gives a clean range: a support floor from summer accumulation between **$62K and $65K** below, and a wall of Long-Term Holder supply between **$83K and $86K** above. Spot is currently pinned inside these two bands.

## Institutional Rails — Muted Turnover
Spot Bitcoin ETFs kept absorbing steady inflows through the rally, with the 7-day average intake reaching **$290M/day**. But secondary trading volume told a different story, holding near **$3B/day** — well below levels seen in earlier expansion phases. Flows concentrated around a single policy catalyst without broader turnover behind them have historically coincided with local highs rather than sustained trends.

## Macro Backdrop — Sovereign Yields Rebound
The Treasury buyback announcement on August 19 briefly pulled the 10-Year yield down toward **4.6%**, but the relief reversed quickly: over the following **eight trading sessions** the yield climbed back to **4.8%**, a fresh cycle high. Sovereign debt pressure remains the dominant force behind global discount rates.

## Transient Equity Decorrelation
During the initial leg of the rally, Bitcoin briefly decoupled from equities as US indices traded flat; the rolling **30-session correlation** between BTC and the S&P 500 dropped toward zero. Historically, such decorrelation episodes around bond-market selloffs tend to be short-lived exhaustion signals rather than a lasting regime change.

## Derivatives and Expiries — Sentiment Cools from Euphoria
The **7-day 25-delta skew** spiked sharply as traders chased calls during the squeeze, then rapidly mean-reverted back to neutral once resistance held. The **180-day skew** stayed flat throughout, indicating long-dated optionality demand is intact even as short-term euphoria faded.

## The September Expiry Barrier
The **September 25 quarter-end expiry** dominates the forward options landscape, carrying roughly **$14B** in open interest across Deribit and IBIT, with heavy concentration at strikes above **$80K** — a significant volatility and positioning anchor into month-end.

## Conclusion
The post-squeeze relief rally has stalled beneath the **$83K–$86K** supply wall. With profit-taking potential now higher at comparable prices than in May, sovereign yields back at cycle highs, and options positioning reset to neutral, the market stays range-bound. The **$62K–$65K** floor remains the key downside reference until the overhead supply is absorbed.
