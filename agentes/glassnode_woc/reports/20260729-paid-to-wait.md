# The Week On-Chain — Paid to Wait

**Fuente:** Glassnode Research · *The Week On-Chain* — https://research.glassnode.com/tag/newsletter/
**Autor:** Frederik Theissen / Glassnode · **Fecha:** 2026-07-29
**Tipo:** newsletter semanal (texto). Cita como `[reports/20260729-paid-to-wait.md]`.

---

## Executive Summary
- The bond market has stopped pricing cuts and leans toward a hike; the FOMC decides the same day this report lands.
- Treasuries out-yield the crypto carry trade, so the marginal bid is parked in cash rather than in this market.
- Spot trades inside the heaviest cost-basis shelf on the chart, **$62K–$68K**, with the **$69K Short-Term Holder Cost Basis** still overhead.
- By depth this is the **shallowest bear on record**, and by the clock it has not yet run as long as its predecessors.
- Exchange flows, spot volume and the ETF bid have gone quiet at the same time — disinterest rather than distribution.
- Hedges were sold at the top of the rally on **21 July** and began coming back within the week.
- Buyers stack bids **2%–20% below spot** while the offer side thins; the Bitcoin Vector reads **Risk Off**.

## Macro — Relative Strength Fades
Last week's story was Bitcoin absorbing an oil shock and finishing ahead of both US and European equities. That edge is gone. Bitcoin slid through this week while stocks sat still, and the decoupling dated from Monday onward — it never got level with equities again. Oil handed back its spike and closed the week the weakest of the four assets. One week does not make a regime, but it removes the one constructive tell of the June repair: Bitcoin being bid on days when equities did nothing.

## Macro — The Bond Market Prices a Hike
The **2-year Treasury yield** has traded above the **Federal Funds Rate since April**, and the spread is now **the widest since November 2022**. That is not a curve waiting for a cut; it leans toward the next move being a hike. A cut today would wrong-foot most of that positioning and would sit naturally next to last week's soft core inflation print. The reaction matters more than the decision: if the market cannot hold a bid on good news it did not expect, that says more about the missing marginal buyer than the FOMC does.

## Macro — Dollar Firm, Bitcoin Handling It Badly
The dollar has rallied **since May**, and Bitcoin is coping worse than in almost any dollar rally on record. Across the precedents **since 2015**, Bitcoin was typically higher by this point in the run; this time it is deep in the red, with only **3 of the 20 past rallies** worse.

## Macro — Treasuries Out-Yield the Basis
The more mechanical channel: the **three-month futures basis** — the yield on the cash-and-carry trade that anchors institutional participation — has been **paying less than a 2-year Treasury since February**. Only **one other stretch on record has run this long, August 2022 into January 2023**, and it ended at the cycle low. When Treasuries out-yield the basis, the desks that supply leverage, depth and volume have little reason to show up. Most of the off-chain picture below reads downstream of that single spread.

## On-chain — Sitting on the Heaviest Shelf
Bitcoin is trading inside the **single heaviest cost-basis cluster in the profile, roughly $62K to $68K**, where more coins last changed hands than anywhere else. The band splits **almost evenly in half**: short-term holders who bought into this year's decline, and long-term holders who have sat through it. The long-term half behaves like patient supply and tends to act as a floor. The short-term half is the reactive one and is mostly underwater, which makes it the first supply to move on a bounce. Overhead, the **Short-Term Holder Cost Basis at $69K** remains the line that decides the next leg, and the next real block of supply above it is the **long-term holder wall between $83K and $86K**.

## On-chain — Shallowest Bear, Unfinished Clock
Two framings agree. Measured against the **200-day moving average**, no prior bear kept price this close to trend — this cycle's deepest discount stopped well short of the mildest previous bear. Measured as a **drawdown from the all-time high**, prior bears bottomed well below where this one has traded. The clock is the other half of it: Bitcoin has spent **about three quarters as long below the 200-day average as the typical prior bear**, and most of those bears ran longer still. A drawdown this gentle has not yet served the time its predecessors served — an argument for patience rather than for calling the low, especially for anyone working from a four-year cycle map.

## On-chain — Flows Becalmed
Exchange deposits and withdrawals have thinned out together and now run at **roughly the same modest pace, among the quietest combined flows of the past three years** and **well below the 2023–2025 norm**. Balances tell it from the other side: they drifted higher off the **April trough** and have been **broadly flat since early July**. That reads less like distribution or accumulation than like disinterest — the pattern that has often marked the quiet middle of a bear. The corollary matters: with so little moving on-chain, there is not much standing supply positioned to absorb a change in demand if one arrives.

## Off-chain — The ETF Flip Did Not Persist
**US spot ETF flows turned positive in mid-July and turned straight back within the week.** Net flows are modestly negative again, and against the redemption waves of **June and early July** the current outflow barely registers. The institutional channel is neither selling this market down nor buying it up. Persistence rather than size was the thing to watch after last week's flip, and it did not persist.

## Off-chain — Hedges Sold at the Top, Bought Back After
The cost of downside protection **collapsed to almost nothing on 21 July**, the same session Bitcoin printed its local high off the June low. Hedges were sold into the top, and the deleveraging that came with the rally left the market leaning into the move that followed — an expensive posture, as it turned out. **Skew has climbed back off that low**, and flow turned with it: the **volume put/call ratio bottomed at its low for the year and has climbed hard** as price slid, with **perpetual funding pinned below neutral all month**. Open interest is the more cautious read — the **put/call ratio on open interest appears to have bottomed at the same moment but has barely lifted off the low**. Until the carried book follows, this is repositioning, not a change of stance.

## Off-chain — Premium Rotates, Volatility Compressed
Options buyers spent the rally paying for upside; since price rolled over a week ago the budget has swung back toward puts, though **one outsized put trade dominates the tail end of that swing**. The real shift is on the call side, where **spending on upside has fallen sharply against last year's pace**. That is rotation, not panic hedging. Volatility pricing agrees: the **whole implied volatility curve is compressed near the bottom of its range**, and the **six-month tenor has almost never been lower** — traders have rarely priced a quieter half-year.

## Off-chain — Volume at Multi-Year Lows
Measured **in coins rather than dollars**, so the price decline does not flatter the series, **spot volume has fallen to its lowest since 2019**. Stripping out Binance — whose **zero-fee promotion inflated tracked volume through 2022 and 2023** — gives a similar shape, though on that cut volume sits above the depths of the last bear. Low volume is less a directional signal than a description of who is left in the room. With cash paid to wait, a good deal of it is waiting.

## Off-chain — Bids Below, Offers Thin
The order book says the money stepped back rather than left. **Since early June a persistent stack of bids has built between 2% and 20% below spot** and has stayed there, refreshed day after day. Above spot the offer side has thinned considerably, with **resting sell orders in the same band near their thinnest of the past month**. Buyers look willing, just not here; and there is little supply standing in the way of a move higher. Thin books cut both ways — they are often how a quiet market becomes a fast one.

## The Vector Agrees
Glassnode's **Bitcoin Vector reads Risk Off** — mild rather than extreme, **one band above capitulation**, in what the model calls a *"tactical pause."* A signal sitting defensive rather than capitulating is the practical form of everything above: the macro regime pays capital to wait, the on-chain and off-chain surfaces have gone quiet, and the model scoring them is not asking anyone to step in front of it. A bounce is unlikely to change that reading; a change of regime would.

## Conclusion
The regime is unchanged and its cause sits upstream of crypto. With **Treasuries out-yielding the basis trade** and the dollar firm, the marginal buyer is paid to stay in cash, and every downstream surface reads that way: **spot volume at its lowest since 2019**, exchange flows becalmed, an offer side that has thinned out. This is the **shallowest bear on record by depth** and, by the clock of past cycles, **unfinished**.

**Confirmation:** improvement starts with policy and shows up as a **reclaim of the $69K Short-Term Holder Cost Basis on returning volume**, with the **ETF channel buying rather than idling**.

**Invalidation:** a **loss of the $62K–$68K shelf, with exchange inflows waking up**, invalidates the constructive case.
