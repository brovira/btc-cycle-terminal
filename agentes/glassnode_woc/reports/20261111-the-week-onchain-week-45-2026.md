# The Week On-Chain — Running on Empty

**Fuente:** Glassnode Research · *The Week On-Chain* — https://research.glassnode.com/the-week-onchain-week-45-2026/
**Autor:** Glassnode · **Fecha:** 2026-11-11
**Tipo:** newsletter semanal (texto). Cita como `[reports/20261111-the-week-onchain-week-45-2026.md]`.

## Executive Summary
- Bitcoin has slipped below the **Short-Term Holder Cost Basis at $75.6K** and lags equities, while the capital that could lift it keeps leaving.
- Stablecoin supply is contracting at the fastest pace since **June 2022**, US spot ETFs are net sellers, and Bitcoin trades at a discount on Coinbase.
- Counterweights: long leverage near price has largely been flushed, bids outweigh asks in the order book, and two large wallet cohorts have begun accumulating.
- The **September high** was the first higher high since the 2025 all-time high; that trend change holds while price stays above the **June low at $58.5K**.

## On-chain — Price Inside the Watched Band
- Last week's level to watch was the **$73K-$76K** band. Price now trades near **$74K**, under the STH Cost Basis (**$75.6K**, average price of buyers within the past **155 days**).
- At the September high nearly all STH coins were in profit; now only about **35%** are, and sellers realize a small loss on average.
- Cost Basis Distribution: about **two thirds** of STH coins sit above price, in three bands bought August-October reaching up to **$83.6K**. Many of these holders may exit at breakeven on a rally (overhead supply).
- Below price the only dense block is **$62K-$66K**, bought around the August low.
- Larger August structure intact: September higher high, price well above the **$58.5K** June low.
- Reclaiming the **True Market Mean at $77.5K** would put about **half** of STH coins back in profit.

## Macro — Bitcoin vs Equities
- Most of the midterm-related decline came before the vote; the sell-off starting **November 5** hit stocks and Bitcoin alike.
- Since **November 9** paths diverged: S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100 recovered above their election-day close, while Bitcoin fell again after the **November 10** CPI release and remains about **5%** below.
- Moving back above Bitcoin's election-day close of **$77.8K** would realign it with equities.

## Macro — Rates
- The 2-year Treasury yield is **4.75%**, **75 bp** above the current Fed rate of **4%**, so the bond market prices further hikes.
- Far from 2022: in June 2022 the same gap peaked at **244 bp**; today's is about a third of that, and it has narrowed since late October.
- Higher rates remain a headwind for risk assets, but no 2022-style repeat is priced.

## Liquidity — Stablecoins
- Total stablecoin supply fell **$14.2B** over 30 days, the fastest contraction since June 2022: redemptions pull cash out of the market, leaving less fuel for a rally. Tether trades slightly below **$1**.
- Trigger to watch: the 30-day change turning back to growth would be the first sign of new money.

## Off-chain — ETF Flows
- US spot ETFs saw **$1.6B** net outflows over 20 sessions; on **November 9** the total crossed the **$1.5B** threshold Glassnode uses to define an outflow stretch.
- Shallow versus past stretches (most went far deeper). Historically inflows returned only after the low, so their return would confirm a low after the fact.

## Off-chain — Coinbase Premium
- Measured with the Tether-below-$1 distortion removed. The 30-day average has been negative through the entire slide from the September high; the discount is the deepest since **February**.
- In past rallies the premium ran positive. A premium turning positive and holding for weeks would signal returning US demand.

## Off-chain — Leverage and Liquidations
- The grind down from the September high has run through most long liquidation levels near price, removing fuel for a forced-selling cascade.
- Largest cluster on the six-month map: **$59.5K-$62.6K**, untouched since the August low; the map is thin between it and price.
- Open interest has rebuilt to its September-high level while price fell about **15%**, suggesting shorts were added into the decline. A turn higher would force short covering; the largest cluster above price is near **$87.5K**.

## Off-chain — Order Book
- Since the November 5 sell-off, resting bids within **5%** of price on Binance and Coinbase have outweighed asks every hour.
- Largest bids: block at **$68K-$72.5K**. Above the nearby asks the book is thin up to about **$84K**, so the book offers little resistance, though STH breakeven sellers remain.
- Caveat: resting orders can be pulled; some bids were pulled during the sell-off and later replaced.

## On-chain — Accumulation Trend Score
- Score near 1 = cohort adding, near 0 = reducing (size-weighted). Over the past week only two cohorts score above **0.5**: **100-1K BTC** and **1K-10K BTC**, both of which distributed through much of October.
- Both also turned to buying in late September and it did not last. All other cohorts (from >10K BTC down to <1 BTC) are still reducing.
- Reading: early dip buying by a narrow group; smaller cohorts joining would show broad buyer return.

## Conclusion
- Bitcoin is correcting with little fresh capital: recent buyers at a loss, lagging stocks, and stablecoins, ETFs and US buyers all showing outflows. Near-term optimism is limited, offset by cleared near-price leverage and two large cohorts buying.
- **Bull/reversal scenario:** for those reading August as the start of a higher-timeframe reversal, this is where mean reversion would begin; a reclaim of the **STH Cost Basis at $75.6K** supports it (next markers: True Market Mean **$77.5K**, election-day close **$77.8K**).
- **Invalidation levels:** a sustained break below **$73K** (floor of last week's band) points to a longer consolidation; a close below the **June low at $58.5K** undoes the August trend change.
- **Confirmation signals listed:** stablecoin 30-day change back to growth; ETF inflows returning; Coinbase Premium positive for weeks; smaller cohorts accumulating.

*Data as of 2026-11-11 11:00 UTC for price and derivatives, 09:00 UTC for the order book; ETF flows, stablecoin supply, cost basis and accumulation scores through November 9.*
