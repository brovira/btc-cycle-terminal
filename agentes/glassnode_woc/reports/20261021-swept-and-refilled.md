# The Week On-Chain — Swept and Refilled

**Autor:** Frederik Theissen / Glassnode · **Fecha:** 2026-10-21

## Executive Summary
- BTC swept to about **$80K on Oct 15**, just under the cost basis of US spot ETFs and corporate treasuries; the bids there refilled within days.
- Overhead, the 6-12 month cost basis, a block of asks and the largest liquidation clusters all sit in a band of about **$85K-$87.5K**.
- ETFs took in **$653M on Oct 19** on thin volume; profit taking has slowed to roughly **half** its pace at the September high.
- Catalysts ahead: Fed decision **Oct 28**; MSCI list of index removals **Nov 11** (Strategy could be on it).
- Altcoin froth and funding have cooled from their highs, but open positions are only slightly smaller.

## Swept at the Cost Basis
- The **US Spot ETF Cost Basis** (average price the ETFs paid), named last week as nearest on-chain support, has been traded around since Oct 8, with several dips under it. Deepest: hourly close of **$80.1K on Oct 15**, followed by an hourly close back above.
- The same line was a cap in spring: price stalled just under it in early May and made no hourly close above it until **Sep 3**. In October it behaves as support.
- **True Market Mean** (average cost basis of active investors) is lower, near **$77.6K**; the sweep stopped well short of it.

### Treasuries Paid About $80K
- Tracked treasury companies hold BTC at an estimated average cost of about **$80.2K**, just under the ETF line. Price spent most of the year below it (over a quarter below at end of June) and has closed above it daily since **Sep 18**.
- Treasury buying has not returned with price: every month since June added less than **a tenth** of the peak month (Nov 2024).
- With both cost bases between **$80K and $81.5K**, the sweep tested two of the largest holder groups at once. A **daily close below $80K** would put both at an average loss.

## Bids Refilled at $80K
- The largest block of bids on the Binance spot book sat at **$80K**, just under the ETF line. The Oct 15 sweep took out most of it; bids were back by **Oct 20**, smaller than at their peak but present.
- Above price, the main ask block sits at **$86.5K-$87.5K**, the first heavy resistance on the book.

### Clusters Shift Above Price
- The liquidation heatmap: the sweep cleared the bright band near $80K. The two largest clusters now sit above price, near **$85K and $87K**. Below price a bright line remains near **$79.6K**, just under the Oct 15 low.
- A move toward $87K could accelerate as shorts are forced out, but it would meet the order-book asks at the same price.

## Still Under the Top Buyers
- The ceiling from last week still holds. Cost basis of coins bought **6-12 months ago: about $86.7K** (down from $88K last week); coins bought **1-2 years ago: about $100K**. The first now sits inside the ask/liquidation zone near $87K.
- Selling from these holders has eased: the 1-2 year group peaked at the start of October (after the September high) and now moves **less than a third** as many coins per day.
- A **daily close above $86.7K** would put 6-12 month buyers back in profit on average and clear the nearest supply band.

## Inflows Return on Thin Volume
- After a week of net outflows, US spot ETFs took in about **$653M on Mon Oct 19**, the largest day since **Sep 22**, turning the weekly flow positive.
- Volume did not follow: that day was below a typical day of the past year, and the 30-day average volume is **under a quarter** of its level a year ago.
- Inflows repeating on rising volume would show demand broadening beyond a single day.

### Profit Taking Slows
- Realized Profit has slowed over the past week to about **half** its pace in the week of the September high. Realized losses are steady; the largest of the week came in the 24h to the morning of Oct 15, as price slid toward $80K.
- A renewed rise in profit taking near **$87K** would be the first sign sellers are back at the top of the range.

## MSCI Sets a Date
- On Oct 15 (after US close) MSCI adopted new rules for "non-operating companies"; a test run on May data flagged Strategy and Metaplanet. An existing constituent is removed only if it fails the test in **both** of its two latest annual filing periods.
- Final list: **Nov 11**; changes effective at close **Nov 30**.
- Strategy shares fell nearly **10% on Oct 16** (worst day since February); BTC barely moved.
- A removal would force index-tracking funds to sell the shares while treasuries already buy far less BTC. If Strategy is on the Nov 11 list, its shares come under pressure first; the next question is whether it keeps buying BTC.

## Oil and Yields Rise Into the Fed
- Fed decides **Oct 28**. Year to date WTI is up about **73%**, household inflation expectations up **0.5 pp**; core inflation is where it started the year, but September CPI beat forecasts on both headline and core (annual).
- The 2-year Treasury yield is up by more than **1 pp** this year to **4.8%**, well above the **4%** top of the Fed's target range, even after the hike on **Sep 16**.
- BTC is up about **10%** since that hike. If oil keeps lifting inflation expectations, a second hike would be harder to rule out.

## Metals Cool, Bitcoin Leads
- Two-month-return leadership (BTC, gold, silver, S&P 500, Nasdaq 100): metals in Q1, US equities through spring into July, **Bitcoin for the past 10 weeks**.
- Tokenized gold (XAUT, PAXG) spot volume peaked mid-February, fell to **15%** of that level by late July, more than doubled into mid-September, and has eased to **20%** of the February peak.
- Binance still has the largest share; OKX and Gate gained most this year; Gate's share went from **0.6% (2024) to 12%** in 2026.
- If metals retake the lead, the February pattern suggests exchange gold trading would rise with them.

## Positions Outlast the Trade
- Tokenized-gold perpetual open interest equals **7.0 tonnes**, **62%** above the February spot peak, up from **5.0 tonnes** in mid-September while spot eased: traders are holding exposure longer.
- Gate added **42%** of the net increase since Jan 1 and climbed from 9th to 2nd in OI ranking since April 2025; OKX (first on the October average) climbed 6 places. Weekly average: Gate **1.7 t** vs OKX **1.5 t**.
- A sustained hold **above 6 tonnes** with metals no longer leading would show gold exposure held on crypto exchanges without a metals rally behind it.

## Most Exchanges Hold Less
- Proof of Reserves, 11 exchanges with at least 1,000 BTC: **six** hold less than a year ago, five grew (Deribit among the fastest). Gate's reserves **+4.9%**; median of the other ten **-6.4%**.
- Spread of declines: four exchanges below their year-earlier level at end of November, eight by end of May, six now. The largest exchange's growth kept the total from falling; the other ten together hold about **10% less** than a year ago.
- Gate's latest PoR (snapshot **Oct 18**): about **$1.4B** more reserves than user holdings; BTC, ETH, stablecoins and every other major asset listed are above **100%**.
- If the declines keep spreading, remaining BTC on exchanges would concentrate at the few still adding reserves.

## Altcoin Leverage Cools
- After BTC slipped to the $80K bids on Oct 15, the median altcoin fell about **4%** over the weekend, its largest weekend drop since May.
- Coin froth (altcoins with heavy leverage vs their own history) peaked **Oct 7** and has left its high zone. Global froth (whole altcoin market) stayed below its high zone and has fallen further.

### Funding Back Near Its Lows
- In late September close to **a quarter** of altcoins paid above-neutral funding; now **fewer than 5%**, near the bottom of the two-year range.
- Open positions are only about **5%** below the October peak at constant prices; a renewed rise in funding would be the first sign of fresh crowding.

## Conclusion
- BTC is still boxed in. The sweep found buyers at the ETF and treasury cost bases (**$80K-$81.5K**), and profit taking has slowed. Overhead, the 6-12 month cost basis, ask block and largest liquidation clusters sit at **~$85K-$87.5K**.
- **Bullish confirmation:** a daily close above **$87.5K** with ETF inflows repeating on rising volume would show demand absorbing the supply.
- **Bearish / invalidation:** a daily close below **$80K** puts the average ETF and treasury holder at a loss and brings the True Market Mean near **$77.6K** into view.
- Upcoming: Fed **Oct 28**; MSCI list **Nov 11**.
