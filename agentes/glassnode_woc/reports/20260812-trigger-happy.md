# The Week On-Chain — Trigger Happy

## Executive Summary
- July core inflation eased a tenth to **2.5%** with the headline flat, equities sit at records, and Bitcoin still faded. A weak response to good news is the warning, not the print.
- Price is wedged between the **Median Realized Price at $63.0K** and the **Short-Term Holder Cost Basis at $68.7K**, on the **lowest spot volume since 2019**.
- The seller side leans constructive: **barely half of circulating supply holds an unrealized profit** (near past bear-floor territory), Seller Exhaustion sits at a cycle low, exchange inflows are fading.
- The buyer side is empty: ETF net flows only just turned positive and remain a fraction of past waves, and coins keep landing on exchanges.
- Leverage is crowded long above a thinning bid — the crowd positioned for a recovery that the demand data does not yet show.
- Two levels frame the week: **$68.7K** overhead as the confirmation trigger, **$58.5K** (June low) below as the invalidation.

## Macro — A Benign Print the Market Refused to Buy
Core inflation came in at **2.5%**, a tenth lower, with headline flat — a benign backdrop with policy static. The Effective Fed Funds Rate has been parked since **December** and still sits **more than a full point above core inflation**, so real rates stay restrictive and the gap only closes as inflation drifts down. The reaction is the story: Bitcoin barely bounced in the hours after the release and equities slipped. A healthy market treats benign inflation as good news; failure to build on it over the following sessions would confirm demand is simply absent.

## Macro — Records Next to Weak Sentiment
Consumer Confidence remains among the weakest readings of the past decade despite two consecutive improvements, while the US Stock Market Index printed a fresh all-time high on **August 7** and holds just beneath it. The contradiction resolves once the driver is named: households expecting higher living costs and a softer economy are rotating out of cash into assets, and the AI trade is where that capital lands.

## Macro — Bitcoin Left Out of the Rotation
Bitcoin has not participated. Spot changes hands at roughly **half the October 2025 peak** and has lagged the equity tape all summer, despite the flight-into-scarce-assets thesis being exactly the case for it. Record equities offer Bitcoin no support until some of that flow rotates back, and the first place a turn would appear is ETF flows — where nothing has appeared yet.

## On-chain — The Cost-Basis Ladder
Spot sits just above the **Median Realized Price at $63.0K** (the level splitting every coin's cost basis down the middle) and below the **Short-Term Holder Cost Basis at $68.7K** (the average entry of the most recent buyers). That recent cohort is underwater, which historically makes it quick to sell into recoveries; the median level has absorbed every test from above for over a month. Deeper down, the aggregate **Realized Price near $52.8K** shows the average coin still holds a healthy profit — the broad market has never been forced into loss this cycle.

Price has spent **nearly three months** inside this pocket and the two levels keep converging as volatility compresses. A sustained **reclaim of $68.7K** flips recent buyers back into profit and opens the first real test of overhead supply; **losing the median leaves little structure before the June lows**.

## On-chain — The Thinnest Tape in Seven Years
Spot Exchange Volume measured in coins (not dollars) has fallen to its **lowest level since the series began in early 2019**, and even **excluding Binance** it now scrapes its **2023 bear-market lows**. Fewer bitcoin are changing hands than at any point in seven years — apathy in its clearest form. A tape this thin amplifies whichever side moves first: modest demand can lift price, modest supply can break it. Participation this low rarely lasts, and it is the classic setup for a volatility expansion.

## On-chain — Sellers Tiring, but No Flush
Two standing bear-market gauges are approaching bottom territory without reaching it. **Barely half of circulating supply holds an unrealized profit**, and June's trough stopped just short of the zone where the **last four bears** ended — each of those floors went deeper. The **Seller Exhaustion Constant** (supply in profit paired with volatility) has ground to a **cycle low, among the weakest readings since 2013**, yet still sits above every prior floor. Sellers are visibly tiring, but the final flush that ended earlier bears has not happened. This remains a "capitulation of boredom rather than pain"; the historical pattern completes either with a dose of pain or with time, and a grind can run far longer than anyone positioned for it expects.

## On-chain — Nine Rejections at Break-Even
**Adjusted SOPR** has spent most of this bear **just below 1.0**, where the average coin moved sells for what it cost. Since the October top the 7-day average has climbed back to that line **nine times**, and **nine times sellers used the touch as an exit**. Overhead supply is still being digested. Both prior bears spent more days below break-even and travelled far deeper before turning, which places this cycle among the milder ones so far. A durable recovery shows up here as the ratio **holding above 1.0 through a rally**.

## Off-chain — ETFs Stopped Selling, but Are Not Buying
Net flows turned positive at the **end of July** for the first time in months, but at a **small fraction of any past accumulation wave**, with the cumulative position still **well below its October peak**. Flows this small can flip sign on a single day's redemptions. The read is apathy: the June outflow wave has exhausted itself (one source of pressure removed), but the institutional bid that powered 2024-2025 has not returned. Every previous recovery began with a visible demand impulse in this chart; there is none today.

## Off-chain — Supply Keeps Arriving
**Exchange Net Position Change** remains in inflow territory, with coins landing on exchanges on most days this year. The pace has faded to a **fraction of its early-June peak** — consistent with the tiring-seller picture — but the direction has held. Against an empty demand ledger, even modest inflows matter: a normal market absorbs them without moving, but on the thinnest tape since 2019 with no ETF bid underneath, the same trickle presses on price.

## Off-chain — Crowded Longs on a Quiet Tape
Derivatives traders show none of the spot market's hesitation. On **Hyperliquid**, the whale book has closed **net long every day since mid-March** — unprecedented in the data's short **one-year history** — with the position peaking in **mid-July** at the top of the local range. A cohort that ran persistently short through late 2025 flipped in March and has pressed the long harder as the range matured. Nothing in the spot or flow data validates that conviction.

The crowding is measurable at the aggregate level, and the metric is a **ratio, not funding or absolute open interest**: **Futures Open Interest now exceeds an entire day of futures volume**, close to the **record set last September**, whereas through **2019-2020 the same book turned over roughly three times a day**. Positions kept growing while participation drained, leaving a large, stale stack of contracts on a quiet tape. The risk is mechanical — when open interest towers over daily volume, liquidations meet little resting flow to absorb them and adverse moves extend further than they otherwise would.

## Off-chain — A Thinning Book Beneath Price
The band of resting bids that framed the summer range **peaked at the start of July and has thinned by roughly a third since**, leaving less support beneath price than at the last test of the lows. The ask side is thin as well, so the imbalance flatters the bulls even as absolute depth erodes. Should the range break, a move toward the **June low near $58.5K** would land on a softer book than the one that caught it six weeks ago, with the crowded longs above supplying the fuel. Thin bids, heavy leverage and record-low volume leave downside moves prone to overshoot.

## Conclusion
A **late-bear compression with an unusual overlay: the crowd positioned for the recovery before the demand arrived**. The seller side leans constructive — supply in profit near past floor territory, seller exhaustion at cycle lows, exchange inflows fading. The buyer side stays empty — minimal ETF flows, the thinnest spot tape since 2019, and Bitcoin lagging equities that sit at records.

**Confirmation scenario:** a reclaim of the **Short-Term Holder Cost Basis at $68.7K on rising volume, with ETF inflows following**.

**Invalidations of the floor case (two, explicitly named):**
1. **Failure to rally on this week's benign inflation data.**
2. **A loss of the $58.5K range low into thin bids and crowded longs.**

Stance: cautious and patient.
