# Macro Special — Pricing Out the Hike

## Executive Summary
- After the Fed's September hike, markets expected another in October. This week that was priced out: odds of an October hike fell from **66% (Monday) to 22% (Friday afternoon)**.
- Drivers: a Fed speaker signalling no urgency, a soft core PCE print and a weak September jobs report. Bitcoin gained only about **1%** over the week.
- Short-term yields fell, long-term yields rose, and the curve steepened back to its pre-hike shape.
- The week's biggest short squeeze came overnight, *before* the jobs report; after the release, Bitcoin slipped and longs were liquidated.
- Options traders did not pay for upside ahead of this report (unlike before the September FOMC); implied volatility fell after the release.

## Macro — Hike Odds Step Down
- Fed funds futures, quarter-point hike at the **October 28** meeting: **66%** Monday → **22%** Friday.
- Tuesday: weak consumer confidence and job openings barely moved odds. NY Fed's Williams ("no need for urgency") took odds from **66% to 50%** within 2 hours.
- Wednesday: softer core PCE took off another **~10 pp**, despite stronger GDP. Thursday: mixed ISM plus Fed Vice Chair Jefferson (more time/data needed) pushed odds lower again. Hot euro-area inflation on Friday barely registered.
- Bitcoin's reaction to each drop was up but small or short-lived: **+0.4%** in the half hour after Williams; the post-PCE jump was given back within **3 hours**; back above Monday's level Thursday afternoon; **+2.1%** overnight into Friday while odds barely moved.

## Macro — Jobs Report
- September payrolls: **29,000** vs **84,000** expected (Dow Jones survey). Unemployment rose to **4.2%**; July and August revised down.
- 3-month average about **51,000/month**, under a third of the 2010s average of **183,000**. Hiring nearly stalled in 2025 (**~10,000/month**) and has only partly recovered.
- Policy tension: weak hiring argues for leaving rates alone; core PCE is **+3.0% y/y** versus the **2%** target, which argues for another hike. Traders leaned toward a hold in October.

## Macro — Yield Curve
- 2-year tracks Fed expectations; 10-year also prices inflation and debt risk. The 2s10s gap was inverted from mid-2022 to late 2024.
- Around the September hike the gap narrowed from about **40 bp to 20 bp** (flattening). This week it steepened back to **42 bp**: 2-year fell on fading hike bets, 10-year rose.
- Steepening via falling short yields usually signals fewer hikes expected, but long-end borrowing costs did not ease: the **10-year was near 5.2%** on Friday.

## Off-chain — Futures Positioning
- In the 24 hours before the report, open interest rose **$2.1B** (Bitcoin was up ~**3%**); in coin terms OI grew ~**2.5%**. Funding stayed positive but below its usual baseline: longs not crowding in.
- OI kept rising for about an hour after the release, then fell **$1.5B** as Bitcoin slid, more than half of what was added since Thursday midday.
- Shorts were ~**60%** of the week's liquidations. Biggest burst at **04:20 UTC Friday**, 8 hours before the report: **$50M** of shorts in 10 minutes, with no data due. The report itself triggered a much smaller squeeze.
- After the release: **$11M** longs liquidated vs **$2M** shorts. By 15:40 UTC Bitcoin was **>1%** below its pre-release level.

## Off-chain — Options and Implied Volatility
- Ahead of big events, 1-week IV tends to rise above 1-month. Before the **September 16** FOMC it stayed above for all 48 hours and the premium vanished within a day. A second spike on **September 21** came with a sudden move (Bitcoin **+2.7%** in an hour).
- Before this jobs report the 1-week line stayed *below* the 1-month for all 48 hours: options traders expected little impact from payrolls.
- Study of **32** jobs reports since 2024: median 1-week IV flat the day before, falling the day after; IV fell after **23 of 32**. Other Fridays drift lower by about half as much, so part of the drop is a normal Friday pattern; the extra jobs-day drop is too small to call reliable.
- This report: within **3 hours** IV fell as much as the median report does in a full day.

## Scenarios and What to Watch
- An October hike is still possible: futures price **22%** for October and about **24 bp** of hikes by December (roughly one quarter-point move).
- Key input: **September CPI on October 14**.
  - Core CPI **above forecast** → October hike becomes more likely again.
  - Core CPI **at or below forecast** → the next expected hike stays in **December**.
