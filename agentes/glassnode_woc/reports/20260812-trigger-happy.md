## Executive Summary
- July core CPI printed **2.5%** (a tenth lower) and equities hit fresh records, yet Bitcoin barely reacted — a muted response to good news reads as a warning sign, not relief.
- Spot is pinned between the **Median Realized Price at $63.0K** and the **Short-Term Holder Cost Basis at $68.7K**, trading on the lowest spot volume since 2019.
- Seller-side gauges are near past bear-floor territory (sellers tiring), but break-even has rejected **nine** separate recovery attempts since the October top.
- Demand is largely absent: ETF inflows are minimal and coins keep landing on exchanges.
- Derivatives traders have positioned long into this vacuum, on a bid that has thinned materially — watch **$68.7K** overhead and **$58.5K** below.

## Macro — Flat Inflation, Flatter Market Reaction
July core inflation eased to **2.5%**, with headline flat, the closest read to target in months. Policy has not moved: the Effective Fed Funds Rate has been parked since December and sits **more than a full point above** core inflation, keeping real rates restrictive. The report's central concern is not the data but the market's shrug — Bitcoin barely bounced and equities slipped in the hours after the print, which the authors read as evidence that demand remains absent rather than as a benign non-event. A healthy market, they argue, should have built on the news.

## Macro — Records Elsewhere, Bitcoin Left Out of the Rotation
Consumer Confidence sits among the **weakest readings of the past decade**, even after two consecutive monthly improvements, while the US Stock Market Index made a fresh all-time high on **August 7** and holds just below it. The report frames this as households moving from cash into assets (chiefly the AI-driven equity trade) on fear of rising costs — flows that have bypassed Bitcoin, which trades at roughly **half its October 2025 peak** and has lagged equities all summer. A turn would first show up in ETF flows, which the off-chain section below finds no sign of yet.

## On-chain — The Cost-Basis Stalemate
Price is boxed between two on-chain levels: the **Median Realized Price at $63.0K** (the midpoint of every coin's cost basis, acting as support from above) and the **Short-Term Holder Cost Basis at $68.7K** (the average entry of recent buyers, acting as overhead resistance since that cohort remains underwater and prone to sell into strength). The two levels have been converging for nearly three months as volatility compresses. Deeper down, the aggregate **Realized Price near $52.8K** shows the broad market still holds a healthy unrealized profit — the market as a whole has not been forced into loss this cycle. A sustained reclaim of **$68.7K** would flip recent buyers back to profit and open the first real test of overhead supply; losing the **$63.0K** median would leave little structure above the June lows.

## On-chain — Activity at a Seven-Year Low
Spot Exchange Volume (measured in coins, not dollars) has fallen to its **lowest level since the series began in early 2019**; even excluding Binance it now scrapes 2023 bear-market lows. The report calls this the clearest available measure of market apathy and notes it has been building for several editions running. A tape this thin cuts both ways: modest demand could lift price sharply, and modest supply could break it just as easily — a classic setup for a volatility expansion, with the rest of the report weighing which side is likelier to move first.

## On-chain — Sellers Tiring, But No Capitulation Flush Yet
Two bear-market gauges sit near (but still above) prior floors: barely **half** of circulating supply holds an unrealized profit, with June's trough stopping just short of where the last four bear markets bottomed. The Seller Exhaustion Constant (which pairs supply-in-profit with volatility) has ground down to a cycle low, among the **weakest readings since 2013**, though it too remains above every prior cycle floor — sellers are visibly tiring, but the report notes the final capitulation flush that ended prior bears has not happened; it characterizes the current drawdown as "a capitulation of boredom rather than pain."

Adjusted SOPR has spent most of this bear just below **1.0** (the break-even line): since the October top, the 7-day average has rallied back to that line **nine times**, and been rejected as an exit point all nine times. Both prior bear markets spent more days below break-even and fell further before turning, placing this cycle among the milder ones so far — a durable recovery would need SOPR to hold above 1.0 through a rally, which has not yet occurred.

## Off-chain — ETF Flows Turn Positive, But Thin
US Spot ETF net flows turned positive at the end of July for the first time in months, though at a **small fraction** of any past accumulation wave, and the cumulative ETF position remains well below its October peak. The report reads this as the June outflow wave exhausting itself rather than a real institutional bid returning — flows this small can flip sign on a single day's redemptions, and every prior recovery in this cycle began with a clearer demand impulse than what shows here today.

## On-chain — Exchange Supply Keeps Arriving
Exchange Net Position Change remains in inflow territory, with coins landing on exchanges on most days this year. The pace has faded to a fraction of its early-June peak (consistent with tiring sellers), but the direction has held — against an essentially empty demand ledger and the thinnest tape since 2019, even this trickle of supply is enough to press on price.

## Derivatives — Leverage Positioned Ahead of Confirmation
On Hyperliquid, the whale book has run net long every day since mid-March — a stretch without precedent in that dataset's short one-year history — peaking in mid-July with price at the top of the local range. This cohort ran persistently short through late 2025 and has pressed the long harder as the range matured, even though nothing in the spot or flow data yet validates that conviction; leverage placed this early concentrates risk exactly where a failed range would hurt most.

At the aggregate level, Futures Open Interest now **exceeds a full day of futures volume**, close to the record set last September (through 2019-2020, the same book turned over roughly **three times a day**). Positions have kept growing as participation drained, leaving a large, stale contract stack on a quiet tape — when open interest towers over daily volume, liquidations meet little resting flow to absorb them, so adverse moves can extend further than usual.

## Order Book — Support Thinning Into the Test
The band of resting bids that framed the summer range peaked at the start of July and has thinned by roughly **a third** since, leaving less support beneath price than at the last test of the lows. The ask side is thin too, which flatters bulls on a relative basis even as absolute depth erodes. Should the range break, a move toward the June low near **$58.5K** would land on a softer book than the one that caught price six weeks earlier, with crowded long positioning above supplying additional fuel for an overshoot.

## Conclusion — Scenarios and Invalidation
The report frames this as a late-bear compression with an unusual twist: positioning (crowded longs, thinning hedges) has run ahead of confirmed demand. Constructive seller-side signals — supply in profit near past floor territory, seller exhaustion at cycle lows, fading exchange inflows — sit against an empty buyer side: minimal ETF flows, the thinnest spot tape since 2019, and Bitcoin still lagging record-setting equities.

- **Bullish confirmation:** a reclaim of the **Short-Term Holder Cost Basis at $68.7K** on rising volume, accompanied by resuming ETF inflows.
- **Invalidation of the floor case:** failure to rally on this week's benign inflation print, or a loss of the **$58.5K** range low into a thin order book and crowded long positioning.

The authors' stated stance: "We remain cautious, and patient."
