## Executive Summary
- Dollar weakness has not translated into Bitcoin strength: the 10-year yield pushing toward **4.7%** keeps financial conditions restrictive and remains the dominant macro brake on price.
- Gold near **$4.4k** and oil in the mid-**$80s** are getting bid while Bitcoin lags, underlining that it is still trading as a liquidity-sensitive risk asset, not an inflation hedge.
- Spot and the Short-Term Holder Cost Basis (**$68.5k**) both sit beneath the True Market Mean (**$75.8k**) — the on-chain definition of a capitulation phase, where recent buyers and the broader active base are both underwater.
- Investor pain is unusually shallow for a capitulation episode: Relative Unrealized Loss peaked near **0.25** vs. readings above **0.6** in prior cycles, while the Realized Profit/Loss Ratio at **0.75** is still above the sub-**0.5** zone that has historically marked seller exhaustion.
- Off-chain signals are mixed: perpetual positioning has turned modestly positive and ETF flows have stabilized off a **-5k BTC/day** trough, but a persistently negative Coinbase Premium and DVOL near the mid-**30s** show spot conviction has not returned.

## Macro — Yields Cap the Dollar's Relief
The DXY has rolled over from its July highs, a setup that would normally help risk assets, yet the 10-year Treasury yield keeps climbing toward **4.7%** and Bitcoin has stayed anchored near cycle lows around **$60–65k**. The report reads this divergence as evidence that elevated real and nominal yields — not the dollar — are the binding macro constraint right now, keeping the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset high. A genuinely supportive backdrop would need dollar softness to be joined by falling yields, not offset by them.

## Macro — Bitcoin Absent From the Hard-Asset Bid
The same divergence shows up against other scarce assets: gold sits near **$4.4k** and oil has recovered into the mid-**$80s**, while Bitcoin remains stuck near **$60–65k** after its earlier drawdown. Glassnode frames this as Bitcoin failing to participate in the broader bid for inflation-sensitive assets, behaving instead like a liquidity-driven risk asset. Closing that relative-performance gap against gold and oil is flagged as a condition for a stronger macro read.

## On-chain — Capitulation Below Both Cost-Basis Lines
The bear-market regime traces back to early February 2026, when price broke below both the True Market Mean and the Short-Term Holder Cost Basis. As the decline extended, the **Short-Term Holder Cost Basis has fallen to $68.5k**, now beneath the **True Market Mean at $75.8k** — meaning current prices sit below the break-even of both recent buyers and the wider active investor base. This configuration is the on-chain hallmark of capitulation, the zone where cycle bottoms have historically formed; it will keep being read as capitulation for as long as price stays under the STH Cost Basis.

## On-chain — A Shallower, More Distributed Drawdown
The market has now spent roughly three months (since mid-May) in this capitulation zone, but the intensity of investor pain has been comparatively mild. Relative Unrealized Loss — aggregate unrealized losses as a share of market cap — **peaked near 0.25 (25%)**, well below the **above-0.6 (60%)** readings seen in prior-cycle capitulations. Glassnode attributes this partly to the shallower drawdown itself, but mainly to supply accumulated around the election period at prices well under the all-time high: overhead supply is more evenly spread rather than clustered near the cycle top. That lowers the average cost basis of in-loss coins and eases aggregate pain — but it also means this overhang needs more time, not a sharper price drop, to clear, which the report ties directly to the current sideways grind.

## On-chain — The Realized P/L Ratio and the Path to Exhaustion
Seller exhaustion, per the report, arrives through extended time in the bear regime, further price discount, or both — and this cycle has delivered a mix of both, with sharp legs down in the second half of January and again in May 2026, and more than seven months now in a confirmed bear market. The **Realized Profit/Loss Ratio** (90-day SMA) has historically dropped below **0.5** before cyclical seller exhaustion is reached; it currently sits at **0.75**, and the report treats any bounce as a local rally rather than a true regime change until the ratio works back up through a **2** threshold.

## Off-chain — Perpetuals Turn Constructive, Spot Stays Absent
The 30-day Perpetual Market Directional Premium has swung back to positive after briefly turning deeply negative during the latest leg down, signaling leveraged traders are again paying up for long exposure — a real but still modest improvement, well short of the extreme premiums seen in past impulsive rallies. In contrast, the Coinbase Premium Index has stayed negative through most of the consolidation even as price stabilized near **$60–65k**, indicating US spot demand has not meaningfully returned. The gap between improving leveraged appetite and absent spot demand is flagged as the thing to watch for confirmation of a genuine recovery.

## Off-chain — ETF Flows Stabilize, Don't Yet Accumulate
US Spot ETF flows have improved materially from the heavy distribution of June/early July, when the 7-day average fell to roughly **-5k BTC/day**. Flows have since repeatedly turned positive, including a strong burst in early August, and the latest reading is marginally positive again after a brief dip back to outflows. Glassnode reads this as institutional selling pressure easing, but not yet as sustained accumulation — a more persistent run of inflows would be needed to confirm rebuilding spot demand.

## Off-chain — Volatility Compresses, Skew Still Pays for Downside
Bitcoin's DVOL has fallen to the mid-**30s**, near the low end of its two-year range, meaning options markets are pricing a fairly subdued near-term volatility outlook despite spot sitting close to recent lows — a compression the report warns can leave the market more exposed to a volatility expansion if a catalyst emerges. 25-delta skew stays positive across the curve (puts still command a premium over calls), but the front end has compressed sharply: the 1-week tenor sits near the low end of its recent range while longer maturities hold near **10–13%**, pointing to eased near-term fear alongside lingering caution further out.

## Off-chain — Two-Way Positioning Around $65K
Recent options premium is concentrated around the **$65k** strike, with large put buying offset by similarly large put selling — two-way positioning rather than a clean directional bet. Further out, call buying clusters near **$68k** and **$130k** (selective upside demand), while put buying near **$45k** shows some participants still hold deep downside protection. Overall positioning reads as risk management around the current spot range rather than conviction in either direction.

## Conclusion
Macro remains the binding constraint: dollar softness is offset by 10-year yields pushing toward **4.7%**, and Bitcoin continues to lag both gold and oil. On-chain, price below both the True Market Mean and the Short-Term Holder Cost Basis confirms capitulation, but a Relative Unrealized Loss peak near **0.25** (vs. above **0.6** in prior cycles) points to a shallower, more distributed drawdown that needs time rather than further price decline to resolve. Off-chain signals are similarly split — perpetual demand positive and ETF outflows stabilizing, against a still-negative Coinbase Premium and implied volatility compressing toward cycle lows. With the Realized Profit/Loss Ratio at **0.75**, seller exhaustion (sub-**0.5**) has not yet been reached; until yields ease and this ratio reclaims the **2** threshold, any bounce should be read as a local rally inside an ongoing bottoming process, not a regime change.
