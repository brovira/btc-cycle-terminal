## Scope
- The web piece is a short overview of the yield routes on Bitcoin; it carries almost no price levels. The only hard figure is the options-premium hit rate below.
- The full report maps **four routes** open to institutional holders: credit and lending, basis and derivatives, volatility and options, and on-chain. For each it estimates what is realistically netted across a cycle and what is given up. Those estimates are in the PDF, not in this summary.

## Route 1 — Futures carry (basis)
- Mechanism: buy spot BTC and sell a futures contract against it, collecting the premium that leveraged longs pay to hold positions.
- Carry has beaten the yield on a short-dated Treasury **only when the market ran hot**; its peaks came in momentum phases.
- For much of this year the carry paid **less than the short-dated Treasury yield**.
- Implication: basis yield is pro-cyclical and tied to leverage demand, so it is weak in calm or defensive regimes.

## Route 2 — Options premium (implied vs realized volatility)
- Mechanism: the option seller is paid for expected volatility (implied volatility, IV) and keeps the difference when realized volatility comes in lower.
- Implied volatility has closed above the volatility Bitcoin then delivered on **nearly 4 days in 5** (about 80% of days).
- The gap (volatility risk premium) is **narrower than its own recent norm**, but it **has not gone away**.
- Described as the steadier of the two derivatives routes.

## Route 3 — Covered calls (how institutions harvest the premium)
- Mechanism: sell a call against BTC already held; the premium is received up front.
- Trade-off: the holder's gain is **capped at the strike price**. The cost is the rally above the strike, which in a strong year is the part of Bitcoin's return that mattered.
- Implication: it works best in range-bound or modestly rising markets and gives up the most in a strong uptrend.

## Takeaways for the agent
- Regime dependence: carry is momentum-dependent (it underpaid Treasuries for much of the year); the options premium is more persistent (IV above realized ~80% of days) but compressed.
- Yield on BTC is never free: each route trades away upside (covered calls), takes leverage-cycle exposure (basis), or takes counterparty and lending risk (credit and lending, not detailed in the web text).
- No explicit scenarios, price targets or invalidation conditions are stated in the web text. Any claim about net yields per route needs the full PDF.
