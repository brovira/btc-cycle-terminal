# The Week On-Chain — Breakdown into Thin Support

**Autor:** Glassnode Research · **Fecha:** 2026-09-16

## Executive Summary
- Bitcoin traded near **$76K**, down about **4.6% on the week**, slipping just under the range held since late August and about **1% below the True Market Mean at $76.7K**.
- The break followed the failed Senate vote on the CLARITY Act (September 15, 2026); the drop is shallow given the news flow, but this is the first daily close below the mean after two prior tests held.
- The Fed decides today (September 16, 2026) with a hike priced in; even without one, real policy is tightening as core inflation falls to **2.4%** against a **3.75%** funds rate (a 1.35-point real gap), and the 2-year yield sits nearly a full point above the funds rate.
- Fresh demand has stalled across every channel: on-chain capital inflows ended a 27-day streak, ETF flows turned negative, stablecoin supply is flat, and corporate treasuries have essentially stopped buying.
- Altcoins sold off harder than Bitcoin around the vote, though the longer uptrend has only bent, not broken yet.
- Options flipped from pricing upside to pricing downside within hours of the vote; a large options expiry on September 25 caps the market with max pain at **$72K** and a call wall at **$85K–$90K**.
- The order book has thinned below spot: most resting bids now cluster within 10% of price, and a lost range points first to the **$71.3K** Short-Term Holder Cost Basis, then to a thin zone down to a **$62K–$65K** on-chain floor.

## Macro — Tightening Without a Move
Core US inflation has fallen to **2.4%**, its lowest since 2021, while the Fed Funds Rate has held at **3.75%** since December 2025. That combination pushes the real policy rate to **1.35 points**, even though the Fed hasn't touched the rate itself — policy is getting tighter passively as inflation cools. The bond market is already pricing further tightening: the 2-Year Treasury yield sits almost a full point above the current funds rate, and a hike is broadly expected at today's (September 16, 2026) decision. Either outcome — a hike or a hold — leaves Bitcoin facing a harder macro backdrop than it had at the range highs two weeks earlier.

## On-chain — Range Lost, Not Yet Confirmed Broken
Price fell under the **True Market Mean at $76.7K** (roughly a 1% breach) and under the range floor it had held since late August, both broken at the hour of the failed Senate vote on the CLARITY Act (September 15, 2026). This is not the first test: the same level was pierced on **August 23, 2026** and **September 10, 2026** and held both times. What's new is that the close on **September 15, 2026** stayed below it — a second consecutive close below would turn the slip into a confirmed breakdown. The next support below is the **Short-Term Holder Cost Basis at $71.3K**, the average cost basis of coins bought in roughly the last five months.

## On-chain — Demand Has Gone Quiet
Realized Cap — the aggregate value of coins at the price they last moved — rose for **27 consecutive days** through September 14, 2026, funding the move into the range. That streak broke: September 15 posted the first daily outflow in 28 days, and the unfinished September 16 reading is also negative. Exchange balances continue to fall (net outflows on the 30-day Exchange Net Position Change), so coins are still leaving exchanges even as the capital that had been absorbing them pauses. US spot ETF flows echo this: after almost **$1B** of inflows in the first days of September, flows turned negative in the week of **September 8–14, 2026**, with roughly **-$334M** net outflow.

## On-chain — No New Fuel in Stablecoins or Treasuries
Stablecoin Market Cap sits near **$301B**, flat on the week and about **4% below its April 2026 peak**; its 30-day growth rate is just under the **1.5%–2.9%** band that has historically preceded Bitcoin's strongest subsequent months (the fastest growth spells, by contrast, tended to precede losses). Growth has recovered from summer's negative readings but hasn't broken to new highs in five months — the sideline cash isn't shrinking, but it isn't building either.

Corporate treasuries, a major buyer through 2025, have essentially exited: net purchases over the past three months total roughly **5.9K BTC**, versus **89K BTC** in July 2025 alone. Their average cost basis (Corporate Treasury Cost Basis) sits at **$80.5K**, about **6% above spot**, putting the cohort underwater. Price has tested that level twice since falling below it in January 2026 — in May and again on **September 3, 2026** — and turned down both times. A reclaim of $80.5K would flip treasuries back to profit and remove one layer of overhead supply; until then it acts as a ceiling.

## On-chain — Altcoins Bend but Don't Break
Measured from the last close before the Senate vote, the median top-100 altcoin fell roughly **two points more** than Bitcoin, and the share of the top-100 trading above its 20-day average collapsed from **56% to 19%** in a single session. The longer trend has held up better: most of the top 100 still trade above their 50-day average and almost none sit at a 30-day low. For comparison, at the May 2026 top the share above the 50-day average halved within a week — a genuine breakdown would look like that, and this week's slip is milder.

## Off-chain — Options Flip to Pricing Downside
Into the vote, the one-week 25-Delta Skew sat well below zero (calls costing more than puts, i.e., positioning for upside). Within hours of the failed vote it crossed above zero and has kept climbing since — a shift toward paying for downside protection. One-week implied volatility spiked into the vote and fell back afterward, and that hedge has not been sold back; a return of the skew below zero ahead of the Fed decision would signal the market buying upside again.

The September 25, 2026 expiry — the largest of the quarter — carries a **max pain at $72K** that hasn't moved up with spot, and its single biggest strike on both sides is **$70K**. Above spot, the book is stacked with calls: the largest strike is **$85K**, with a second wall at **$90K**, both sitting inside or just above the **$83K–$86K** ceiling this report flagged last week from long-term holder supply — spot and options markets agreeing on where upside is capped.

## Order Book — A Thin Floor Below
Resting bids have pulled in toward price: nearly **two-thirds** of bids within 20% of spot now sit between **1% and 10%** below it, up from about half at the start of the year, while the buy wall that previously stood in the 10%–20%-below zone has drained well under its normal depth. In price terms, near-side bids reach down to about **$68K**, below which the book is thin until roughly **$61K** — a "no man's land." The **$71.3K** STH Cost Basis sits inside the near-bid block, so a retrace stopping there would be a routine test; if those bids are exhausted, the next on-chain floor is **$62K–$65K**, where the heaviest block of supply below the market last changed hands.

## Conclusion — Scenarios and Invalidation
The range has failed at the bottom but the break is not confirmed: price sits about **1% under the $76.7K True Market Mean** after a shallow drop through a heavy news week. The bull case rests on resilience (a comparably small decline given the CLARITY Act failure and a looming Fed hike); the bear case is the absence of any incoming demand — on-chain and ETF inflows have stopped, stablecoin supply is flat, treasuries are underwater and inactive, altcoins have rolled over, and options now pay for downside beneath a wall of calls.

- **Bull / range-restoring scenario:** two consecutive daily closes back above **$76.7K** combined with Realized Cap growing again.
- **Bear / breakdown-confirming scenario:** a second daily close below **$76.7K**, after which the **$71.3K** STH Cost Basis and the **$62K–$65K** on-chain floor determine how deep the retrace runs.
