# The Week On-Chain — A Rally Running Light

**Autor:** Glassnode · **Fecha:** 2026-10-07

## Executive Summary
- Volume stays unusually thin and fresh capital explains under two fifths of the Realized Cap rise (**~$4.9B** of new money vs **~$12.8B** of Realized Cap growth over 30 days to Oct 5).
- Recent buyers (STH) sold into the first close above **$85K** (Sunday Oct 4): **~86%** of that day's exchange inflows, the highest share in a year.
- Options sentiment has turned bullish: put/call ratios below 1 and in the lower third of their 2026 range; call premium outspends put premium.
- Liquidation levels mostly sit below price (nearest **$81.7K-$83.3K**), but the biggest one-year cluster is above, heaviest near **$92K**.
- Sellers keep stacking asks above price; the largest Binance bids sit at **$81K**.
- Every US data release this week faded in Bitcoin while the S&P 500 held; the US session is now a net seller.
- Altcoins led into late September but now lag, with leverage stretched.

## Volume and New Money
- Total Bitcoin volume (spot exchanges plus US spot ETFs) averages **~$6.8B/day** (7-day), below **9 in 10** days since Jan 2024.
- No day since **Sept 22** has printed a normal spot volume for its weekday; Sunday's close above **$85K** came on roughly **half** a normal Sunday's volume.
- A rise in spot volume would be the first sign of buyers returning in size.
- Over the 30 days to Oct 5: ETF flows + stablecoin growth + corporate treasury buying ≈ **$4.9B**; Realized Cap rose **~$12.8B**. New money covers **< 2/5** of the rise; the rest is coins trading at higher prices among existing holders. 2024 and 2025 rallies had a similar mix but far larger inflows. Until inflows recover, the move relies on holders paying up.

## Profit-Taking at the Breakout
- Oct 4 was the first daily close above the **$85K** wall (ETFs closed for the weekend).
- **~86%** of coins sent to exchanges that day came from short-term holders (<**155 days**) in profit; a typical day is under **2/5**. Highest in the past year.
- If STH keep depositing at this pace, the next push toward $85K meets the same supply.

## Options Positioning
- Put/call ratios (open interest and volume) both **< 1** and in the lower third of 2026 ranges; OI ratio ≈ **0.56** puts per call.
- Premium: puts led on **234 of the last 365 days**, but the picture flipped after mid-August. On a 30-day average, calls outspend puts by **~$17M/day**, about half the widest gap on the chart.
- Read as optimism building; it can become froth if price stalls while traders keep buying upside.
- History is non-directional: in **13** episodes since 2020 with short-dated calls this pricey vs puts and volatility this cheap, Bitcoin was higher a month later in **7**.

## Liquidation Heatmaps
- Two-month map: only **~17%** of levels sit above price; the shelf around price grew **~50%** in a week.
- Downside clusters: nearest **$81.7K-$83.3K**; second near **$75K**; heaviest band **$60K-$63K**. A move down into them could be amplified by forced long liquidations.
- One-year map: the largest cluster above runs **$87.1K-$95.9K**, heaviest near **$92K**; half of it built since late March as shorts accumulated above the range.
- A push through the **$86.5K** ask block could trigger short-covering and extend a move higher. Until then, the nearer clusters below matter more.

## Order Book (Binance Spot)
- The **$85K-$85.5K** ask wall from last week was traded through on Sept 30; price closed above $85K on Oct 4 and Oct 5.
- New asks appeared at **$86.5K-$86.75K**; price turned back under on Oct 6 and early Oct 7 broke through the bids at **$85K**.
- Largest bid block now **$81K-$81.25K** (standing since Oct 3), with the liquidation cluster just above it: **$81K** is the level to watch if the slide continues.
- A settled close back above **$85.5K** would recover the level lost this week.

## Macro and Trading Clock
- All three US releases this week: early rise in Bitcoin, faded within 12 hours, while the S&P 500 stayed above its pre-release level. After PCE, Bitcoin gave back an early **+2%** jump; after the Oct 2 payrolls it was **-2.3%** twelve hours later.
- Next test: CPI on **Oct 14**. Last FOMC minutes due late **Oct 7** (after this edition's data).
- From July to the Sept 21 breakout, most gains came in US hours. Since the breakout the US session is a **net seller** and all net gains came outside it, including the weekend that carried the >$85K close.
- Until the US session turns positive, the rally rests on thinner overnight/weekend trading.

## Altcoins
- In each of the two prior weeks ~**7 in 10** altcoins beat Bitcoin; in the latest week fewer than **3 in 10** did.
- Leverage has not cleared: the share of large caps with open interest high vs market cap (relative to own 1-year history) is the **highest since the run-up to the Oct 2025 crash**; mid caps are stretched too. Part of this is price falling with positions left open; continued declines could force an unwind.
- Median altcoin beta fell back toward **1** and the median coin now trails BTC. With beta near 1, a further BTC slide hits alts at least as hard; alts beating BTC again with rising beta would signal rotation resuming.

## Conclusion
Bitcoin spent the week around **$85K** lacking volume, new money and US-session demand for a lasting breakout; recent buyers sold the move, options turned bullish, and liquidations and bids stack below price. The market leans on positioning more than fresh demand.

**Scenarios / invalidation:**
- **Constructive:** higher spot volume and ETF buying plus a settled close above **$85.5K** would show real support and bring the **~$92K** cluster into reach.
- **Bearish:** a break of the **$81K** bids opens the liquidation clusters below (**$81.7K-$83.3K**, **$75K**, **$60K-$63K**); with altcoin leverage stretched, the wider market would feel it.
